# Personal Positioning

## 1. Selected Positioning Statement

I help FinTech teams working with document-heavy lending and compliance-sensitive workflows make document extraction, verification, and review more reliable, traceable, and audit-ready — through structured document intelligence.

---

## 2. Alternative Positioning Statements Considered

### Alternative 1: Narrow Document Extraction Specialist

*Helping FinTech lending teams extract and verify structured data from financial documents.*

Not selected because it collapses the positioning into a single pipeline stage — extraction — rather than the broader workflow problem of making document-heavy processes reliable and auditable. This framing risks being read as an implementation contractor for one step rather than someone who understands why that step matters to the business. It also offers the least long-term flexibility: it doesn't naturally extend into compliance, KYC/KYB, or audit conversations, which are exactly the adjacent areas the current proof of work and domain research support.

### Alternative 2: Lending Document Intelligence

*Document intelligence for lending and underwriting workflows.*

Not selected despite strong commercial clarity and a direct connection to a well-evidenced pain point (financial spreading and underwriting bottlenecks). The gap is fit with current proof of work: existing production experience is in contract-heavy document systems, not lending origination specifically. Locking the positioning to lending alone would also narrow out compliance, audit, and regulatory document workflows — areas where the current work is arguably a closer match.

### Alternative 3: Broader Reliable AI Systems for Operational FinTech Workflows

*Reliable AI systems for broader operational FinTech workflows.*

Not selected because it claims territory — prediction, fraud detection, anomaly detection, automated decisioning — that current proof of work does not support. It reads as generic AI consulting rather than a specific, credible wedge, and would weaken a first outreach conversation by giving a prospect no concrete reason to believe the problem is understood before a call even starts.

## 3. Why This Audience and Problem Are Specific Enough

### Audience specificity

"FinTech teams working with document-heavy lending and compliance-sensitive workflows" is specific because it names a workflow reality the audience already recognizes in their own day-to-day operations — messy statements, inconsistent filings, manual cross-checking — rather than describing them by attributes external to their work. Internal ICP filters like 50–150 employees, growth stage, UK/US geography, funding round, or CTO/Head of AI title are useful for deciding *who to research and contact*, but they are not language a person recognizes themselves in. Nobody thinks of their own team as "a growth-stage company between 50 and 150 employees" — they think of it as a team drowning in loan documents that don't extract cleanly. Keeping the ICP filters internal, for targeting, and using workflow language externally, for positioning, is what makes the outreach feel personalized rather than templated.

### Problem specificity

The problem — unstructured, inconsistent financial documents that make extraction, cross-referencing, verification, and review slow, error-prone, and difficult to audit — is concrete because it names each stage where the pain actually shows up, not just a vague "documents are hard" claim. It connects directly to costs a decision-maker already tracks: analyst and reviewer time, turnaround time on applications or audits, rework from transcription errors, review burden on compliance teams, and the compliance risk created when outputs can't be traced back to a source. This is a problem a CTO, Head of AI, or Founder can immediately place inside their own operations, which is what makes it usable for personalized, problem-led outreach rather than a generic AI pitch.

### Narrow enough for outreach

Because the problem is tied to specific workflow stages rather than a broad category like "AI adoption," outreach can reference a company's actual document types, review processes, or compliance obligations — which is exactly the personalization the outreach campaign already depends on to earn a reply.

### Broad enough for adjacent work

The wedge doesn't lock into lending alone. Because it's framed around document-heavy and compliance-sensitive workflows generally, it extends naturally into underwriting, KYC/KYB, audit, and regulatory reporting conversations — all workflows sharing the same underlying reliability and traceability problem — without stretching into unrelated territory like fraud scoring or credit prediction, where current evidence and proof of work are weak.

## 4. Proof of Work Supporting the Claim

### Already Available

- A production AI Contract Management system built and deployed for a UK client, involving document parsing and structured handling of contract HTML documents.
- A dynamic table editor for structured document editing, resolving real production challenges around DOM normalization, rowspan/colspan handling, and reactive form typing.
- A bidirectional/sequential paragraph-matching algorithm for cross-document text comparison, used for contract text selection — a direct, evidenced instance of cross-document comparison and evidence-linking logic.
- Production frontend engineering experience building human-facing review interfaces around AI-assisted outputs, integrating structured data into usable workflows rather than raw model output.

These capabilities are transferable to financial document workflows because the underlying engineering problems are the same — unstructured or semi-structured source documents, structural normalization, cross-document matching, and human review interfaces — even though the deployed system was a contract management product, not a financial services product. This is proof of the *pattern*, not proof of a finance-specific deployment, and the positioning is written to reflect that distinction honestly.

### Currently Being Built

- Active specialization in FinTech AI, including domain research into financial workflows, underwriting, compliance operations, and the economics behind them.
- Deeper research into auditability, evidence traceability, and reliability patterns specific to financial document workflows (schema enforcement, grounding, human-in-the-loop review).
- Public technical writing and content — including work on single vs. multi-agent architectures and the shift from prompt engineering to tool-first system design — building visible domain credibility ahead of outreach.
- Development of the financial document intelligence direction as a market-entry wedge, including the outreach campaign and ICP research already underway.

This is research, positioning, and early-stage direction-setting — real and relevant, but not yet a shipped technical artifact in the financial domain.

### Still Needed

The highest-value missing proof is a financial-document-specific prototype demonstrating structured extraction, schema validation, cross-document verification, evidence traceability, appropriate confidence or exception handling, and a human review step. This is what closes the gap between "I have built production document systems" and "I can apply these capabilities to financial document workflows" — right now that gap is bridged by transferable pattern-matching, which is credible but not the same as direct evidence. A focused prototype, even a small one built against public filings or sample loan documents, converts the claim from plausible to demonstrable.

## 5. Three Differentiating Skills or Insights

### Differentiator 1: Production Document Workflow Engineering

Real experience building complex document systems — structured table extraction and editing, cross-document text matching, and human-facing review interfaces — in a live production environment, not an isolated demo or notebook exercise. This matters because most of the hard problems in financial document intelligence aren't modeling problems, they're workflow and data-structure problems: inconsistent layouts, structural normalization, and making AI output usable inside a real review process. Having already solved versions of these problems in production is a stronger signal than having built a single extraction demo, because it shows the messier, less glamorous engineering work has actually been done before.

### Differentiator 2: Reliability-Oriented AI Engineering

An engineering approach centered on structured outputs, schema validation, evidence traceability, cross-document verification, and human review around probabilistic AI — rather than treating model output as an end result. In financial workflows, a plausible-looking answer isn't good enough; it has to be traceable back to a source and defensible under review, which is why this reliability orientation is more relevant here than general AI fluency. This is a developing specialization built on real engineering habits already present in current production work, not a claim of having implemented every reliability technique in a financial deployment.

### Differentiator 3: FinTech Workflow and Domain Understanding

A developed understanding of lending, underwriting, and compliance-sensitive operations — including auditability, explainability, operational handoffs, and regulatory constraints — and, critically, where AI should automate a step outright versus where it should support a human decision versus where deterministic logic is the only appropriate tool. This connects technical implementation to workflow economics and operational risk, which produces sharper problem framing than approaching FinTech as a generic AI application domain, and it's what allows outreach and conversations to speak credibly to how a prospect's team actually works, not just what a model can technically do.

## 7. Short Introduction / Outreach Paragraph

Document-heavy lending and compliance workflows tend to break in the same place: statements and filings that don't extract cleanly, numbers that need to be cross-checked across sources, and outputs that need to hold up under review or audit — not just look plausible. My production experience is in closely related document-engineering problems — structured document handling, cross-document text comparison, structured data extraction, and building human-facing review interfaces that keep people in the loop rather than trusting AI output blindly. I'm now applying and extending those capabilities specifically toward financial document workflows, with the goal of making extraction, verification, and review more reliable and traceable for teams working in lending and compliance. If that's a problem your team is running into, I'd be glad to compare notes on where the actual bottleneck sits for you.
